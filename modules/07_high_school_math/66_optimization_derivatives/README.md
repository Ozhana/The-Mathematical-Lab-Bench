\# Lesson 66: Optimization with Derivatives



\### 📗 The Mathematical Essence (High School Level)

Optimization is the process of finding the maximum or minimum value of a function. The derivative ($f'(x)$) tells us the slope of the function. When the slope is \*\*zero\*\*, we have reached a peak (maximum) or a valley (minimum).



\*\*The Process:\*\*

1\. Define your Cost or Profit function $f(x)$.

2\. Take the derivative: $f'(x)$.

3\. Set $f'(x) = 0$ and solve for $x$ (The Critical Points).



\---



\### 🌍 Real-World Applications \& Examples



\#### 1. Minimum Material Packaging

Imagine you are a soda company. You want to make a can that holds exactly 330ml but uses the \*\*minimum\*\* amount of aluminum to save costs. Derivatives help you find the exact radius and height to minimize surface area.



\#### 2. Maximum Profit in Business

If you lower the price of a game, you sell more copies. But if the price is too low, you lose money. Derivatives help companies find the "Sweet Spot" price that maximizes total profit.



\#### 3. Fuel Efficiency in Aviation

Pilots and flight computers calculate the optimal cruising altitude where fuel consumption is minimized. This "valley" in the fuel-consumption curve is found using calculus.
