# Lesson 15: Volumes of Revolution (The Disk Method)

### 📗 Theoretical Framework
In integral calculus, a **Volume of Revolution** is a solid figure obtained by rotating a 2D curve around an axis. This is a fundamental concept in mechanical design and fluid dynamics.

**The Disk Method Formula:**
If a function $f(x)$ is rotated about the x-axis from $a$ to $b$, the volume $V$ is given by:
$$V = \pi \int_{a}^{b} [f(x)]^2 dx$$

### 📝 Engineering Challenge: Designing a Parabolic Reflecting Cone
Find the volume generated by rotating $y = \sqrt{x}$ around the x-axis from $x=0$ to $x=4$.
1. **Integral Setup:** $V = \pi \int_{0}^{4} (\sqrt{x})^2 dx = \pi \int_{0}^{4} x \, dx$.
2. **Analytical Solution:** $V = \pi [\frac{x^2}{2}]_0^4 = 8\pi \approx 25.13$ cubic units.

### 💻 Computational Approach
Using **Matplotlib's 3D toolkit (mplot3d)**, we simulate the rotation by generating a meshgrid in cylindrical coordinates. This allows us to "see" the solid that the integral is measuring.
